# Pensions: allowances and options

This guide sets out the main pension rules for the 2026/27 tax year. It is general information, not personal advice. Decisions about pensions, especially transfers and how to draw an income, should be taken with advice.

## Annual allowance

The annual allowance is £60,000. It is the most that can be paid into your pensions in a tax year with tax relief, including employer contributions. For high earners the allowance tapers down to a minimum of £10,000. Unused allowance from the previous three tax years can be carried forward.

## Money purchase annual allowance

Once you have taken taxable income flexibly from a defined contribution pension, the money purchase annual allowance of £10,000 applies to further contributions.

## Tax-free lump sum

Up to 25% of a pension can normally be taken tax free, subject to the lump sum allowance of £268,275 across all your pensions. Lump sums above the allowance are taxed at your marginal rate.

## Self-invested personal pension

We offer a self-invested personal pension (SIPP) that can be managed on a discretionary or advisory basis. The SIPP accepts transfers from other personal pensions and from defined contribution workplace pensions.

## Transfers from defined benefit schemes

Transferring out of a defined benefit (final salary) scheme is a significant decision. Where the transfer value is more than £30,000, the law requires you to take regulated advice before the transfer can proceed. Our starting position is that a defined benefit pension is usually best left where it is; we will only recommend a transfer if we can show it is clearly in your interest.

## Drawing an income

From age 55 (rising to 57 from April 2028) you can draw from a defined contribution pension through flexi-access drawdown, buying an annuity, taking lump sums, or a combination. Each has different tax consequences; your adviser will model them with you.

## Death benefits

Pensions can be passed to beneficiaries you nominate. Please keep your nomination form up to date, especially after marriage, divorce or the birth of children.
