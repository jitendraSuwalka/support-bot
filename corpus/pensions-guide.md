# Pensions: allowances and options

This guide sets out the main pension rules for the 2026/27 tax year and the changes already announced for April 2027. It is general information, not personal advice. You may wish to take advice before making decisions about pensions, particularly transfers and how to draw an income; free, impartial guidance is also available from Pension Wise, part of MoneyHelper.

## Tax relief on contributions

Your own contributions receive tax relief up to 100% of your relevant UK earnings in the tax year, or £3,600 gross if your earnings are lower. Basic-rate relief is added to the pension automatically; higher- and additional-rate taxpayers claim the extra relief through their tax return.

## Annual allowance

The annual allowance is £60,000. If the total paid into your pensions in a tax year, including employer contributions and the value of any defined benefit accrual, exceeds it, an annual allowance charge is normally due. For high earners the allowance tapers down to a minimum of £10,000. Unused allowance from the previous three tax years can be carried forward, provided you were a member of a registered pension scheme in those years.

## Money purchase annual allowance

Once you have taken taxable income flexibly from a defined contribution pension, the money purchase annual allowance of £10,000 applies to further defined contribution contributions.

## Tax-free lump sum

Up to 25% of a pension can normally be taken tax free, subject to the lump sum allowance of £268,275 across all your pensions. Lump sums above the allowance are taxed at your marginal rate.

## Our pension

Our self-invested personal pension is administered by our SIPP operator partner and can be managed by us on a discretionary or advisory basis. It accepts transfers from other personal pensions and from defined contribution workplace pensions.

## Transfers from defined benefit schemes

Transferring out of a defined benefit (final salary) scheme is a significant decision. Where the transfer value is more than £30,000, the law requires you to take appropriate independent advice from a firm with the Financial Conduct Authority permission to advise on pension transfers before the transfer can proceed. Fernhill Wealth holds that permission. The Financial Conduct Authority expects advisers to start from the assumption that a transfer will not be suitable, and we recommend one only where we can show it is clearly in your interest.

## Drawing an income

From age 55, rising to 57 from 6 April 2028, you can draw from a defined contribution pension through flexi-access drawdown, buying an annuity, taking lump sums, or a combination. Each has different tax consequences. When you apply to access a pension we will refer you to Pension Wise and offer to book a free guidance appointment for you, as the rules require, unless you have already had one or have received regulated advice.

## Death benefits

You can nominate the people you would like to receive your pension when you die by completing an expression of wish form; the scheme administrator takes it into account but it is not binding. Please keep it up to date, especially after marriage, divorce or the birth of children.

- If you die before age 75, benefits are normally paid free of income tax, within the lump sum and death benefit allowance of £1,073,100. If you die at or after 75, the recipient pays income tax at their marginal rate.
- From 6 April 2027, most unused pension funds and death benefits will be included in the value of your estate for inheritance tax. Benefits passing to a spouse or civil partner, or to a registered charity, remain exempt, and dependants' scheme pensions and death-in-service benefits are outside the new rules.
