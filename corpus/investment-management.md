# Investment management service

## Discretionary and advisory

With discretionary management, you agree an investment mandate and risk level with us and our investment managers make the day-to-day decisions within it. With advisory management, we recommend changes and you decide whether to make each one. Most of our clients choose discretionary management because it allows us to act promptly.

## Risk profiles

Portfolios are managed to one of seven risk profiles, from 1 (lowest risk, mainly cash and short-dated bonds) to 7 (highest risk, almost entirely equities). Your profile is agreed with your adviser using a questionnaire and a conversation about your capacity for loss, and is reviewed at every annual review.

## How portfolios are built

- A long-term strategic allocation between equities, bonds, property and alternatives for each risk profile, set by our Investment Committee and reviewed annually.
- Active and passive funds selected by our research team on cost, process and performance consistency.
- Rebalancing whenever an asset class drifts more than 5 percentage points from its target, and at least every six months.

## Responsible investment

Each risk profile is available in a responsible version that excludes companies deriving significant revenue from tobacco, controversial weapons and thermal coal, and tilts towards companies with stronger environmental and social practices. Fees are the same as for the standard portfolios.

## Reporting

You receive a quarterly valuation report showing holdings, performance against the agreed benchmark, transactions and fees, published to the client portal within 15 working days of each quarter end. Your investment manager is available to discuss it.

## Where your assets are held

Assets are held by an independent custodian in a nominee account in your name. They are ring-fenced from the firm's own assets, which means they are not available to the firm's creditors.

## Risk warning

The value of investments and the income from them can fall as well as rise, and you may get back less than you invested. Past performance is not a reliable guide to future returns.
