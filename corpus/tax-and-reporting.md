# Tax on your investments and tax reporting

This guide covers the main UK taxes on investments held outside an ISA or pension in the 2026/27 tax year, and the documents we provide for your tax return. It is general information, not personal advice.

## Capital gains tax

Gains on investments sold in a general investment account are subject to capital gains tax above the annual exempt amount of £3,000. Gains above the exempt amount on shares and funds are taxed at 18% or 24%, depending on your total taxable income. Losses can be set against gains in the same year or carried forward if reported to HMRC.

## Dividends

Dividends are tax-free within the dividend allowance of £500 a year. Above the allowance, dividends are taxed at 10.75% for basic-rate taxpayers, 35.75% for higher-rate taxpayers and 39.35% for additional-rate taxpayers.

## Interest

Interest on cash and on bond funds is taxed as income. A personal savings allowance may cover some or all of it, depending on your rate of tax.

## ISAs and pensions

Income and gains within an ISA or pension are free of UK income tax and capital gains tax. The ISA guide and the Pensions page explain the allowances and the changes due from April 2027.

## Moving investments into an ISA

Selling holdings in a general investment account and buying them back within an ISA, often called a Bed and ISA, uses your ISA allowance and may realise a capital gain. There is no dealing charge for a Bed and ISA on our model portfolios.

## Documents we provide

- A consolidated tax certificate, published to the portal by the end of May each year, showing dividends, interest and tax deducted for the tax year.
- A capital gains report, showing realised gains and losses and the figures needed for the capital gains pages of a tax return.
- The annual costs and charges statement, which is not a tax document but is often requested alongside them.

We do not complete tax returns, but your adviser can introduce you to an accountant if you need one.

## Important information

This page is general information, not personal advice. Tax treatment depends on your individual circumstances and may change in the future. The value of investments and the income from them can fall as well as rise, and you may get back less than you invested.
