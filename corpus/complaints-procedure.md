# How to make a complaint

We want to know if something has gone wrong, so that we can put it right. There is no charge for making a complaint, and making one will not affect the service you receive.

## How to complain

Complaints can be made by letter, telephone, email or in person, and may be raised with anyone in the firm. The Client Resolution team can be reached:

- by email at complaints@fernhillwealth.example;
- by telephone on 0117 496 0355, 09:00 to 17:30 on working days;
- by post to Client Resolution, Fernhill Wealth, Fernhill House, 22 Harbourside Walk, Bristol, BS1 5XY.

If someone is complaining on your behalf, we need your written permission before we can discuss your affairs with them.

## What happens next

1. If we can resolve your complaint to your satisfaction within three working days, we confirm this in a summary resolution letter, which also explains your right to go to the Financial Ombudsman Service.
2. Otherwise we acknowledge your complaint in writing within five working days and tell you who is handling it.
3. We investigate, and may contact you for more information.
4. We send a final response letter setting out what we found, whether we uphold the complaint, and any redress, together with the Financial Ombudsman Service's leaflet.
5. If the investigation cannot be completed within eight weeks of receiving your complaint, we write to explain the delay, when we expect to respond, and your right to go to the Financial Ombudsman Service without waiting.

## The Financial Ombudsman Service

If you are dissatisfied with our final response, or if eight weeks pass without one, you can refer the matter free of charge to the Financial Ombudsman Service. You must do so within six months of the date of our final response.

- Telephone: 0800 023 4567, or 0300 123 9123
- Website: www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk
- Post: Financial Ombudsman Service, Exchange Tower, London, E14 9SR

## Complaints about events some time ago

The Ombudsman can normally consider a complaint made within six years of the event, or within three years of when you became aware, or ought reasonably to have become aware, that you had cause to complain, whichever is later. If you are unsure whether a complaint is in time, contact us anyway.
