# Cash, income payments and dealing

## How cash is held

Cash in your accounts is held as client money in pooled client bank accounts with our partner banks, separate from the firm's own money, under the Financial Conduct Authority's client money rules. Interest is paid on cash balances at the rates shown on the portal; we retain a margin of 0.25% a year between the rate we receive and the rate we pay, which is disclosed in your client agreement.

## Regular income and withdrawals

You can take a regular income from a general investment account or ISA monthly, quarterly or annually, paid on the 25th of the month to your verified bank account. Income can come from the natural income of the portfolio, from selling holdings, or both; your adviser will discuss which approach suits you.

## Regular contributions

Regular contributions by direct debit can be set up from £250 a month and are invested in line with your agreed portfolio on the next dealing day after receipt.

## Dealing and settlement

We deal in funds and shares on each working day. Fund purchases and sales are placed at the fund's next valuation point and settle within four working days; share trades settle one working day after dealing. Money from a sale can be paid out once settlement has taken place.

## Corporate actions

Where a company or fund you hold announces an event such as a rights issue, takeover or fund merger, we handle it on your behalf under a discretionary mandate, and write to you with the options under an advisory mandate. We do not vote at company meetings on your behalf unless you instruct us to.

## Tax on cash interest

Interest earned on cash outside an ISA or pension is taxable and is reported on your annual consolidated tax certificate. From 6 April 2027 a charge will also apply to interest paid on cash held inside a stocks and shares ISA; the ISA guide explains this.

## Important information

This page is general information, not personal advice. Tax treatment depends on your individual circumstances and may change in the future. The value of investments and the income from them can fall as well as rise, and you may get back less than you invested.
