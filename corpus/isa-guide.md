# ISAs: a guide for clients

This guide is general information about the rules for Individual Savings Accounts in the 2026/27 tax year. It is not personal advice. Whether an ISA is right for you depends on your circumstances; your adviser can discuss this with you.

## The allowance

The overall ISA allowance is £20,000 for the 2026/27 tax year, which runs from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. It can be split in any proportion between cash ISAs, stocks and shares ISAs, innovative finance ISAs and a Lifetime ISA. Any unused allowance does not carry forward.

## Lifetime ISA

A Lifetime ISA can be opened by anyone aged 18 to 39. Up to £4,000 a year can be paid in, counting towards the £20,000 overall allowance, and the government adds a 25% bonus of up to £1,000 a year. Withdrawals other than for a first home or after age 60 incur a 25% government charge.

## Change from April 2027

From 6 April 2027 the amount that savers under 65 can pay into a cash ISA each year is capped at £12,000. The overall £20,000 allowance is unchanged, so the remainder can still go into a stocks and shares ISA. Savers aged 65 or over on 6 April keep the full £20,000 cash ISA allowance. Money already held in cash ISAs is not affected.

## Transfers

You can transfer an ISA between providers without losing its tax status, provided the transfer is made through the new provider's transfer process rather than by withdrawing the money. Transfers of stocks and shares ISAs to Fernhill Wealth typically complete within 15 working days; transfers that involve selling holdings may take longer. We do not charge for transfers in or out.

## Flexible ISAs

Our stocks and shares ISA is flexible: money withdrawn during a tax year can be replaced in the same tax year without using more of your allowance.

## Tax

Interest, dividends and capital gains within an ISA are free of UK income tax and capital gains tax. ISAs form part of your estate for inheritance tax, although a surviving spouse or civil partner can inherit an additional allowance equal to the value of the ISA.
